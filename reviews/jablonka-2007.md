## Chapter and ESF boundaries

The chapter treats Jablonka and Lamb as central intellectual foundations while labeling its extension to reconstructed developmental circumstances as ESF interpretation. Its evidence categories distinguish established mechanisms, contested hypotheses, ESF synthesis, and prospective tests. The revised card follows this approach.

Developmental persistence within an exposed individual does not demonstrate transmission between organisms. Cellular inheritance through division does not demonstrate germline inheritance. Parental physiology, learning, routines, institutions, and shared material conditions can affect successive generations without transmission of an acquired molecular state through gametes.

Any inherited-stress mechanism needs an operational definition of the proposed signature, exposure and outcome measures, route-specific evidence, and appropriate generations. A claim of germline transgenerational transmission additionally needs persistence beyond direct exposure and controls for genetic differences, renewed exposure, and social/environmental routes. Neurotype validity and cooperative benefits need independent empirical tests. This review does not adjudicate the entire contemporary human inheritance literature.
