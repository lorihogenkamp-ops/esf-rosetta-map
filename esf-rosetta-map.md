### Claim 1 -- Stress is part of adaptive regulation, not merely damage.

*Opening · Strong convergent support*

In ESF, stress names information-bearing adaptive work through which living systems anticipate, meet, update to and recover from demand. Stress can become costly or destabilizing, but it should not be reduced to damage, distress or overload.

**Boundary:** Do not imply that every demand is harmful or that every stress response is beneficial.

### Claim 13 -- Regulatory capacity must be studied across demands, context and time, not treated as a universal score.

*Environment and methodology · Methodological implication*

Before asking how much capacity someone has, ask: capacity for what, under which conditions, at what cost and over what period? Population averages can obscure meaningful differences in response, thresholds, burden and recovery. Regulatory Bandwidth names the organism's present coordinated regulatory capacity across interacting stress-responsive systems. ESF investigates this capacity through response-and-recovery profiles across specified challenges and conditions, alongside population research and comparisons with strong alternative models. Any scalar summary must identify the represented system, challenge, evaluation criterion, protected function or viability endpoint and time horizon.

**Boundary:** Population methods and scalar summaries remain useful within justified scopes. Declaring conditions makes a score interpretable, not validated. A response profile does not, by itself, identify regulatory architecture.
