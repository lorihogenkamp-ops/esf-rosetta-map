# Errata -- ESF Rosetta Stone Map v1.0 (16 Oct 2026) to v2.0.0 (2026-10-26)

**If you downloaded or cited version 1.0, please use this list.** Every one of the 50 v1.0 citations was checked against the Crossref record for its DOI (first pass 17 Oct 2026; second pass including article numbers and author names, 26 Oct 2026). 14 citations were wrong in at least one element: six gave the wrong journal, one cited a non-existent journal record for what was then a preprint, and the rest had a wrong volume, issue, article number, page range, or a truncated title. The remaining 36 matched. Corrected entries are marked in the map ("citation corrected") and keep their v1.0 wording in the data file (`full_v1`) for traceability.

## 2. Annotation revisions

Four v1.0 annotations overstated what their source shows. The v1.0 text is kept in the data file (`*_v1` fields); the map shows the revised reading with an "annotation revised" marker.

**Bobba-Alves et al. (2022)**  
*Retired:* 'Regulatory bandwidth is measurable and finite' (supports line) and 'Use as empirical anchor for ESF Paper 3's Regulatory Bandwidth construct' (next line)  
*Now:* The paper is a narrative review developing an energetic model of allostatic load. It supports the cost side of regulation and does not measure or validate Regulatory Bandwidth. Evidence type corrected from empirical study to review.

**Nijhof et al. (2025)**  
*Retired:* The v1.0 card (under Sosenko et al. 2024) described a general argument for an autism-specific multimorbidity index  
*Now:* The published study compares multimorbidity indices for predicting COVID-19 mortality among 1,027 hospitalised autistic adults and proposes a three-predictor index that outperformed the Quan index (AUC 0.872 vs 0.828). Read as an example of scoped, outcome-specific measurement, not as validation of regulatory coupling.

**Miot et al. (2023)**  
*Retired:* 'Supports one of the framework's strongest claims: chronic conditions often cluster ... because they are expressions of interacting system states'  
*Now:* The study reports clustering and subgroups in a cohort of 63 autistic adults with intellectual disability at one time point. Association and clustering are kept distinct from an ESF mechanism of within-person coupling.

**Mahony & O'Ryan (2022)**  
*Retired:* Evidence type empirical study (v1.0 implicit)  
*Now:* Conceptual framework: mitochondrial allostatic load as a proposed, untested mediator. Supports lines kept in their hedged form.

## 3. Structural changes that affect how v1.0 should be read

- The 14 map claims of March 2026 (c01-c14) are no longer a claim set. They are kept as navigation topics with their history; the Field Guide's 14 Public Claims (v1.0, 17 Oct 2026) are the only numbered claims.
- Two March 2026 claims are retired as wording: c02 (superseded by Public Claim 4, which no longer equates emergent allostasis with health) and c11 (cooperative neurodiversity, now a supplementary proposition from the Terminology and Boundary Map, not a child of Public Claim 7).
- Source-to-claim connections are no longer undifferentiated 'supports' lists. Each is typed (supports / constrains / refines / challenges / background / develops), scoped, and carries a review status. In v2.0.0 all 175 typed links are PROPOSED and none is counted. The 128 v1.0 topic links are kept as history; 19 of them are marked as retired support readings.
- 'Evidence Layers 1-6' are now 'Evidence Domains E1-E6' (ESF Constitution, Article III), so that 'Layer 1/2/3' is free for its canonical meaning (architecture / calibration / expressed profile).
- Seven ESF publications (Papers 1, 2A, 3, 5; Evidence Ecologies; the dementia working paper; and Schworer et al. 2022 as a scoped external card) are added. The ESF papers carry a 'develops' relation only: developed-in is not supported-by.

## 4. What has not changed

The 50 v1.0 sources are all still in the map, in the same six evidence domains, with their March 2026 annotations unless marked revised. Each source keeps its v1.0 topic assignments as history. The v1.0 record on Zenodo (16 Oct 2026) remains available unchanged as the historical baseline under the same concept DOI.
